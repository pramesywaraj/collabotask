# Product Requirements Document — CollaboTask

> **What this doc is:** the **product** — what CollaboTask is, who it's for, what it feels like to use, and how it should behave from the user's side. It is the upstream document: it defines the product; the spec translates it into technical contracts.
> **What this doc is *not*:** API contracts, database schema, SQL, WebSocket message formats, or implementation rationale. Those live in [001-software-specifications.md](./001-software-specifications.md). Testable acceptance per feature lives in [001-user-stories.md](./001-user-stories.md).
>
> **Status:** Living document. **Owner:** pramesywaraj. **Audience:** solo dev today; written so a future teammate understands the product from this doc alone.

## 1. The Product in One Paragraph

CollaboTask is a **Trello-like Kanban app for small teams**. You create a workspace, invite your team, open a board, and drag cards across columns to track work. The board is **alive**: when a teammate moves a card or someone joins, you see it happen instantly — no refresh, no stale state. It is deliberately simpler than Jira: the entire learning curve is "open a board, drag a card." Over time, it aims to grow into a home for a team's working rhythm — eventually supporting agile rituals like daily standups.

---

## 2. Why CollaboTask Exists

### 2.1 The problem
Small teams need to coordinate work, but the usual tools force a bad trade-off:

| The pain | What teams settle for today | What CollaboTask does instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Too heavy** | Jira and enterprise tools demand setup, configuration, training | You're productive in your first minute — open a board, drag a card |
| **Feels dead** | Lightweight tools are single-player; you refresh to find out what changed | The board updates live as teammates act; you see who's here right now |
| **Murky permissions** | "Who can edit this board?" is unclear, or it's all-or-nothing | It's always clear who can do what — and private work stays private, but never stranded |

### 2.2 What we believe (product principles)
These are the convictions the product is built on:

1. **Simplicity is the feature.** Every screen should be obvious. If a user has to think about how the tool works instead of their work, we failed.
2. **Collaboration should feel live.** Seeing a teammate's change the moment it happens is not a "nice extra" — it's the core reason to use a shared board instead of a spreadsheet.
3. **Authority should be clear, never bureaucratic.** People should always know who's in charge of what, and getting access should never require begging or red tape — while sensitive work still stays controlled.
4. **Build it like it's real.** Even where we defer features, we never ship a confusing or half-thought-out experience. Every "not yet" is a deliberate choice, not a gap we forgot about.

## 3. Who It's For

### 3.1 Primary audience
**Small collaborative teams (roughly 2–15 people)** who manage shared work and want it lightweight: startups, squads, project groups, side-project collaborators. The long-term aim is to serve teams who work in a cadence — sprints, standups, recurring rituals.

### 3.2 The two people we design for

**The Organizer** (team lead / workspace admin)
- *What they do:* set up the workspace, invite the team, create boards, decide who sees what.
- *What they want:* to be in control without friction. They should be able to step into any board when they need to — but feel confident that doing so on a private board leaves a clear trace.
- *What frustrates them today:* tools where being "admin" still means clicking through permission hoops, or where they can't tell who has access to what.

**The Doer** (team member / contributor)
- *What they do:* live inside a board or two, move their cards, see what teammates are working on right now.
- *What they want:* to capture a task and drag it without thinking, and to feel the board is shared and alive — not a static list they have to refresh.
- *What frustrates them today:* setup walls before they can start, and never knowing if what they're looking at is current.

### 3.3 The jobs they hire CollaboTask to do
- *"When I start a project, I want to spin up a board and get my team working on it within a minute."*
- *"When a teammate moves a card, I want to see it move — so we're never out of sync."*
- *"When I open a board, I want to instantly know who else is here and what's changing."*
- *"As the lead, I want real authority over every board without bureaucracy — but I want our private boards to stay private, with an honest record of who opened them."*

## 4. What It's Like to Use

This section describes the **experience** — what the user does and what they feel — not the mechanics behind it.

### 4.1 Getting started (first run)
A new user signs up and lands in their workspace immediately. They create a workspace, give it a name, and invite teammates by email. There's no configuration to wade through — the next natural step is "create your first board," and the board comes pre-set with **To Do / In Progress / Done** so there's a working Kanban from the very first second. *Time from sign-up to a usable board: about a minute.*

### 4.2 A board, alive
Opening a board shows columns of cards and **who else is currently here** (presence). From this moment the board is shared in real time:
- A teammate drags a card to "Done" → it moves on your screen too, right away.
- Someone adds a card, renames a column, joins the board → you see it happen.
- You drag a card → it moves instantly under your cursor (it feels immediate), and everyone else sees it land where you dropped it.

The promise: **what you see is always what's true, for everyone, right now.** Nobody refreshes to "catch up."

### 4.3 Organizing work
- **Columns** are the stages of work; teams rename them, add new ones, reorder them to match how they actually work.
- **Cards** are the work items — a title, optional description, an assignee, a due date. Drag them within a column to prioritize, or across columns to show progress.
- Everything is drag-and-drop and feels instant.

### 4.4 Control & clarity (the permission experience)
This is where CollaboTask is opinionated, because "who can do what" is where team tools usually get confusing.

- **Workspace admins are genuinely in charge.** An admin has full authority over *every* board in their workspace — they never have to be "added" to a board to manage it. Being on a board is about *involvement*, not *power*.
- **Boards have one clear owner.** Every board has exactly one accountable owner (whoever created it, until they hand it off). There's never ambiguity about who's responsible. Ownership can be **transferred** cleanly — it moves from one person to another, it's not a vague "co-owner" pile.
- **Private boards stay private — but never stranded.** A board can be workspace-visible (everyone on the team can see and open it) or private (only its members). An admin can always *see* that a private board exists, but to actually open its contents they **join first — one click, and it's recorded.** So sensitive work stays controlled, and "an admin looked inside" is always honest and visible.
- **No orphans.** If a board's owner leaves, the board doesn't break or lock up — admins still fully manage it, and a new owner can be named when it suits the team.

The feeling we're after: **you never have to wonder who can do what, and you're never blocked by needless gatekeeping — yet private work is genuinely protected.**

### 4.5 Managing the team
Admins invite and remove members, and can promote a teammate to admin (or step one back down) so authority can be shared as the team grows. People can leave a workspace they're no longer part of. The product quietly protects against footguns — e.g. a workspace can't be left with no one in charge.

## 5. Scope: What We're Building (and What We're Not)

> The authoritative, itemized scope lives in [001-software-specifications.md §8](./001-software-specifications.md). This is the **product-intent** summary.

### 5.1 In Phase 1
- **Accounts:** sign up, log in, basic profile.
- **Workspaces:** create, invite/remove teammates, share or step back admin authority, leave, delete.
- **Boards:** create them (workspace-visible or private), browse them, open them, edit their settings, archive them, manage who's on them, join them, and transfer ownership.
- **Columns & cards:** create, rename, reorder, delete — all drag-and-drop.
- **Live collaboration:** presence (who's here) plus every board change reflected to everyone in real time.
- **A quiet activity record** behind the scenes (see §6).

### 5.2 Deliberately *not* in Phase 1
Each of these is a conscious "not yet," not an oversight:

| Not yet | Why we're waiting |
| --- | --- |
| Comments on cards | Valuable, but the core board + live collaboration has to feel great first |
| Labels / tags | Additive polish; doesn't change the core experience |
| File attachments & avatar uploads | Needs a whole file-storage capability — defer until the core earns it |
| Public boards (shareable link) | Anonymous, link-based access is a different trust model; revisit later |
| Account deletion / deactivation | A real obligation when we have real users; planned, not forgotten |

### 5.3 Explicit non-goals
- **Not a Jira replacement.** No epics, story points, or heavy process — *yet* (see Vision, §6). Simplicity is the point.
- **Not an org/billing platform.** No multi-tenant billing or enterprise org management.
- **Not a native mobile app** in Phase 1 (responsive web is the target).

## 6. Where This Is Going (Vision)

CollaboTask's ambition is to become **the place a team's working rhythm lives**, not just a task cupboard. The clearest direction:

- **Support for agile rituals.** A daily-standup view ("what each person moved yesterday, what they're on today"), sprint-style boards, and eventually planning/retro support. This is the north star that makes CollaboTask more than a Trello clone.
- **A team's history, made useful.** Comments and richer collaboration around cards.

**One product decision we're making now because of this vision:** even though there's no activity-history *screen* in Phase 1, the product **records team activity from day one**. We're choosing to capture the team's working history early so that, when the standup and ritual features arrive, the story of how the team worked is already there — not starting from an empty page. This is a deliberate investment toward the future above, and the single reason an otherwise-invisible feature is in Phase 1.

## 7. What Success Looks Like

CollaboTask is both a portfolio piece *and* a potential real product, so success has two faces.

### 7.1 Phase 1 is "done" when
- A team can do the whole loop end-to-end: sign up → workspace → invite → board → columns → cards → drag things around → **and watch it all update live for everyone**.
- The permission experience behaves exactly as described in §4.4 — control is clear, access is frictionless, private boards are protected and auditable.
- The app feels **production-quality**, not a prototype: nothing half-built, every deferral intentional.

### 7.2 If/when real users arrive, we'd watch
*(Leading indicators — not gates for shipping Phase 1, but how we'd judge it as a product.)*
- **Time-to-first-board** — how fast a new user gets from sign-up to a working board (target: a few minutes). Tests our "simplicity" promise.
- **Collaboration rate** — share of boards with more than one active member. Tests whether the "team" premise holds.
- **Live engagement** — sessions with two or more people on a board at once. Tests whether "alive" is actually felt and valued.
- **Return rate** — teams coming back across a week. The real signal that it's useful.

## 8. Assumptions & Open Questions
- We assume teams are **small** (the product is tuned for that; large-org scale is out of scope for now).
- We assume **email is a fine identity** for Phase 1 (no single-sign-on yet).
- **Open:** when public/shareable boards arrive, what's the right trust and visibility model? (Deferred — flagged so it's a deliberate future decision.)
- **Open:** which agile rituals matter most to our target teams — standup first, or sprint planning? (To learn from real users.)
